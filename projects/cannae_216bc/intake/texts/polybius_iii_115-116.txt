Lucius Aemilius fell in the thick of the fight after 
receiving several dreadful wounds, and of him we 
may say that if there ever was a man who did his 
duty by his country both all through his life and 
in these last times, it was he. The Romans as long 
as they could turn and present a front on every 
side to the enemy, held out, but as the outer ranks 
continued to fall, and the rest were gradually huddled 
in and surrounded, they finally all were killed where 
they stood, among them Marcus and Gnaeus, the 
Consuls of the preceding year, who had borne 
themselves in the battle like brave men worthy of 
Rome. While this murderous combat was going on, 
the Numidians following up the flying cavalry killed 
most of them and unseated others. A few escaped 
to Venusia, among them being the Consul Gaius 
Terentius, who disgraced himself by his flight and 
in his tenure of office had been most unprofitable 
to his country.
