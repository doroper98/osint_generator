When the news reached Rome that the armies 
were encamped opposite each other and that engage- 
ments between the outposts occurred every day, 
there was the utmost excitement and fear in the 
city, as most people dreaded the result owing to 
their frequent previous reverses, and foresaw and 
anticipated in imagination the consequences of total 
defeat. All the oracles that had ever been delivered 
to them were in men’s mouths, every temple and 
every house was full of signs and prodigies, so that 
vows, sacrifices, supplicatory processions and litanies 
pervaded the town. For in seasons of danger the 
Romans are much given to propitiating both gods 
and men, and there is nothing at such times in rites 
of the kind that they regard as unbecoming or 
beneath their dignity.
